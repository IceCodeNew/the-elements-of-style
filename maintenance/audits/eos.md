## Conflicts found

1. **End emphasis vs bottom line up front.** EOS-R18b puts the emphatic sentence or paragraph at the end of a paragraph or composition. Tech norms (bottom line up front, the inverted pyramid, JA 木下是雄 重点先行 and 結論先行) put it first. Essay norms in all three languages (EN climax, JA 起承転結 and 尾括型, ZH 卒章显志) agree with Strunk. Resolved by **PERSONAL**, and writing-tech states the opposite rule.
2. **Topic sentence first vs JA essay structure.** EOS-R09 opens each paragraph with its point. JA personal essays (起承転結, 尾括型) and narrative paragraphs, which Strunk himself exempts, do not. Resolved by **TECH**.
3. **Comma splice vs ZH and JA punctuation.** EOS-R05 forbids joining independent clauses with a comma. ZH (GB/T 15834-2011) separates the clauses of a 复句 with ， and JA joins clauses with 、. Resolved by **EN**. The ZH counterpart fault (一逗到底) belongs in writing-zh.
4. **One tense in summaries vs JA and ZH tense systems.** EOS-R17 forbids shifting tense. ZH has no grammatical tense, and JA narration alternates る and た forms by norm. Resolved by **EN**.
5. **Combine sentences vs 一文一義.** EOS-R13d merges a step-by-step chain into one sentence (the Macbeth example ends up with a 26-word sentence). JA 一文一義 and tech procedures that give one action per step favor short sentences. Resolved by a **conditional BOTH**: merge only when sentences restate a referent to carry one idea, and keep one idea per sentence. 一文一義 itself goes to writing-ja.
6. **Inanimate subject as the cure vs translationese.** EOS-R10d revises toward an inanimate subject with a transitive verb ("Failing health compelled him to leave college"). JA treats 無生物主語 as 翻訳調, and ZH treats the same pattern as 欧化. Resolved by **EN**.
7. **"Very" vs ZH 很.** EOS-V60 says to use "very" sparingly. In ZH, 很 before a predicative adjective is often grammatically required filler, not emphasis. Resolved by **BOTH** with the condition "if an intensifier adds nothing", plus a 很 caveat in writing-zh.
8. **"Etc." after "such as" vs JA and ZH lists.** EOS-V22 calls "etc." after "such as" incorrect. ZH 「如…等」 and JA 「例えば…など」 are standard. Resolved by **EN** for the "such as" rule and **BOTH** for the completeness rule (EOS-V22b).
9. **Thanking in advance.** EOS-V58 rejects it. JA 「よろしくお願いいたします」 and ZH 「提前感谢」 are standard closings. Resolved by **EN**.
10. **Generic "he" vs singular "they".** EOS-V59 requires "he". Modern guides (Chicago, AP, APA 7, M-W, the Google and Microsoft style guides) accept singular "they" and discourage generic "he". Resolved by **EN**, with the rule reversed.
11. **House-style punctuation.** EOS-R01 (Charles's) and EOS-R02 (serial comma) agree with Chicago and disagree with AP (Charles'; no serial comma). Resolved by **EN** keyed to house style.
12. **Modals in specs.** EOS-V06 (can vs may) is dated for general prose but current in specs (ISO/IEC Directives Part 2, RFC 2119), so it is resolved by **EN/TECH**. EOS-V50 (shall/will) conflicts with requirements usage, where SHALL means MUST and plain-language guides prefer "must"; it is resolved by **EN**, marked dated.
13. **Padding words vs tech terms of art.** EOS-V07 (case), V24 (factor), V25 (feature), V26 (fix), V57 (system) and V14 (data is) conflict with established software terms (test case, two-factor, feature flag, bug fix, operating system, "data is"). Resolved by **EN** with a terms-of-art exception.
14. **No fragments vs labels and voice.** EOS-R06 forbids fragments. Tech conventions (commit subjects, headings, UI labels, list items) and personal voice use them legitimately. Resolved by **EN** with conditional wording.
15. **Vary vs parallel, inside the source.** EOS-R14 asks the writer to vary monotonous sentences and EOS-R15 asks for similar form. Strunk scopes them apart: R15 applies to co-ordinate content, and R14 applies to unrelated thoughts forced into the same two-clause shape. Both stay **BOTH**, and each rule should state its scope.
16. **Definite assertions vs politeness.** EOS-R11 asks for definite assertions. JA and ZH personal and business writing use politeness hedges. Resolved by a **conditional BOTH**: hedge where uncertainty is real or politeness is expected, and do not hedge to evade. Politeness formulas belong in writing-ja and writing-zh.
17. **Causal "as".** EOS-R04c recommends "as" for "because". Some modern tech style guides advise against causal "as" because it can read as temporal (not re-verified). Resolved by **EN**: prefer "because" or "since".
18. **Skill wrapper vs source.** EOS-SK01, SK02 and SK07 ("ANY prose", "cut ruthlessly", condensed absolute titles) contradict the source's own qualifications (EOS-R10, R13, R18). Resolved by **X-SCOPE / X-DUP**, importing the conditional forms from the rule rows instead.

## Language-specific examples to move

### EN

- Punctuation rules EOS-R01 to R07 with their examples: Charles's and its; serial comma and the firm-name exception; paired parenthetic commas, restrictive vs non-restrictive "which/who"; the comma before a co-ordinating conjunction; semicolons between independent clauses and before therefore/thus; the "No reply." fragment; dangling participles ("Being in a dilapidated condition, I was able to buy the house very cheap").
- EN realizations of the universal rules:
  - EOS-R10: the Boston and Restoration-dramatists pair.
  - EOS-R10b: the gold-export double passive.
  - EOS-R10c: survey → surveyed; mobilization → mobilized.
  - EOS-R10d: "there is", "could be heard".
  - EOS-R11b: not honest → dishonest, with the meaning-shift warning.
  - EOS-R13: the padding table, the "the fact that" table, "who is" and "which was", and the Macbeth example.
  - EOS-R14: the concert paragraph.
  - EOS-R15: textbook vs laboratory method; articles and prepositions in a series; correlatives.
  - EOS-R16: the Wordsworth and Bessemer examples; "only"; "All the members were not present"; relative-pronoun placement.
  - EOS-R18: the fortitude and razor-steel pairs; inversion ("Through the middle of the valley flowed a winding stream").
- Padding words (principle EOS-R13): case, character, nature, system, factor, feature, student body, respective/respectively, possess, he is a man who, as to whether. Each needs the terms-of-art exception.
- Intensifiers (principle EOS-V60): very, certainly, so.
- Stock phrases (principle EOS-V00): along these lines, one of the most, interesting (as preamble), lose out.
- Confusable pairs that are still valid: affect/effect, less/fewer (with the round-number exception), compare to/with, who/whom, consider (no "as"), divided into/composed of, literally, while (temporal ambiguity), as good as or better than, most/almost, don't/doesn't, can/may (specs only).
- Dated entries to drop or mark "conservative usage only": data, dependable, different than, due to, folk, fix, get/gotten, however (sentence-initial), near by, prove/proven, shall/will, would/should, split infinitive, they (reversed), viewpoint, worth while, bid/bade, claim, clever, all right (the senses part), lose out, like/as (informal).

### ZH

The source has no ZH examples. The universal rows need these ZH analogs, which writing-zh should state as its own rules:

- EOS-R02: 顿号 、 between list items, with no mark before 和/或.
- EOS-R05: ， between the clauses of a 复句 is standard (GB/T 15834-2011); the fault is 一逗到底.
- EOS-R07: 「通过…使…」 (缺主语).
- EOS-R10: overuse of the 被 construction.
- EOS-R10c: 「进行/加以/予以 + 动词」 nominalizations.
- EOS-R10d (opposite direction): inanimate-subject causatives read as 欧化.
- EOS-R15b: 关联词 position relative to the subject (不仅…而且).
- EOS-R17: no grammatical tense; time words carry it.
- EOS-V07: 「在…的情况下」 as padding.
- EOS-V22 and V22b: 「如…等」 is standard; in 「A、B、C等三项」, 等 closes the list.
- EOS-V31: 「值得一提的是」, 「有趣的是」.
- EOS-V60: 很 is often grammatical filler, not an intensifier; the real intensifiers are 非常 and 特别.
