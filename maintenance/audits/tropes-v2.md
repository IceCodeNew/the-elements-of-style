Conventions:

- Destinations follow `.claude/skills/updating-upstream-rules/rubric.md`. A TV row is classified on its merits, even when a candidate rule already covers it; the `Candidate rule(s)` column then names that rule. `X-DUP` is used only inside these two sources: the whip restates all 49 tropes as imperatives, so a WH row that says the same as a TV row is `X-DUP (TV-nn)`, and its notes say what the whip changes (usually an absolute ban).
- `Candidate rule(s)`: candidate rules whose behavior would prevent the anti-pattern. The first one is the best fit. "(partial)" means the rule covers only part of the pattern.
- `Check`: the global check in `evals/global-checks.json` that tests the anti-pattern. For an excluded row (X-DEAI) the check stays, as the owner decided, because the pattern is a symptom that candidate rules may prevent.

## Conflicts found

1. **Em dashes.** TV-02 allows 2–3 per piece; WH-T02 bans dashes for pauses, asides and pivots; zh.dash-form prescribes the 破折号 "——" for 解释说明 and 插入语; JA uses "――". Resolution: X-DEAI. The global check is `general` and counts only pause, aside and pivot dashes.
2. **Wh- headings.** TV-23 and WH-T17 ban them; tech.headings-name-content uses "Why Promote returns early" as a good heading, and FAQs and JA 「なぜ〜か」 headings are norms. Resolution: X-DEAI; the check targets teaser headings only.
3. **Counting.** WH-B04 bans every explicit count. That conflicts with numbered procedure steps (tech.one-action-per-step), counts that are facts ("41 zip files") and counts the reader needs ("takes two arguments"). Resolution: X-DEAI; the check exempts these.
4. **Self-echo vs synonym cycling.** TV-15 says do not reuse your words; TV-20 says repeat the noun. Resolution: both.define-and-keep-terms keeps a defined term the same, and the global check trope-self-echo scores a figure that recurs without new meaning.
5. **Point first vs building to a point.** TV-05, TV-06 and WH-B02 say the point comes first. pers.end-on-the-point puts an essay's point in the last paragraph. Resolution: BOTH with a text-type condition (requests, replies, updates and expository paragraphs lead; an essay may build to a verdict it states once).
6. **Summaries.** WH-B07 bans any closing restatement; both.say-it-once keeps a summary at the start or end of a long document; JA 「まとめ」 and ZH 「综上」 are norms in tech and academic text (zh.stock-closing keeps a contentful 综上). Resolution: TV-28 BOTH with the long-document condition; TV-49 PERSONAL.
7. **Reasoning in the output.** WH-R03 ("Give the answer, not your reasoning … never in end-user communications") conflicts with tech.specific-change-summary (name the specific reason), design-doc rationale and error text that names a cause. Resolution: BOTH, conditioned on the reader's need (both.write-for-the-reader).
8. **The whip contradicts itself.** WH-01 asks the writer to state in the output which rule it broke and why, which is the reasoning leak that WH-R04 and WH-B01 ban. Resolution: WH-01 is X-SCOPE.
9. **Quotes and arrows.** TV-36 and WH-T28 require straight quotes; zh.full-width-punct requires “ ” and ja.kagi-quotes requires 「」. Resolution: X-DEAI; no quote check.
10. **"We".** TV-25 targets "we"; tech.neutral-register recommends "We recommend…" in design docs. Resolution: BOTH, conditioned on the group including the writer and having acted or decided.
11. **Ornate nouns vs terms.** TV-31 and WH-T24 list "framework", "ecosystem" and "gated", which are literal tech terms (tech.established-terms). Resolution: BOTH for figurative uses only.
12. **Anaphora vs 排比.** TV-33 targets repeated openings; ZH rhetoric values 排比 in essays and speeches, and both.parallel-form asks for parallel list items. Resolution: BOTH limited to running prose; the 排比 case is not fully resolved (see summary).
13. **Analogies.** WH-T27 allows an analogy only when asked; explanatory writing uses analogies that map a known structure. Resolution: TV-35's condition (both.write-for-the-reader).
14. **"Imagine" vs reader-in-scene.** TV-38 bans "Imagine a world where…"; pers.reader-in-scene asks for the reader's situation shown from inside. Resolution: no real conflict: the scene shows a present situation at the material's strength, and "Imagine a world" sells a hypothetical future.
