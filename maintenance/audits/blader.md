# Rule audit: blader/humanizer v3.1.0

Source: blader/humanizer @ `8c307f367c91bd974002f10f2be2965d642c13df` (v3.1.0, 2026-10-16), upstream of op7418/Humanizer-zh.

Audited: `SKILL.md` (every line) and the rules in `AGENTS.md`. `CHANGELOG.md` and the git history were read for context only. `agents/openai.yaml` (display name and default prompt) and `scripts/validate-package.py` (package checks) contain no writing rules.

**IDs.** The prefix `BH-` stands for blader humanizer. `BH-Pnn` is SKILL.md pattern §nn. A letter suffix (`BH-P20a`) means one pattern was split because its parts go to different destinations. Other prefixes: D = description, I = intro, A = account ("Why AI text sounds…"), C = core rules, W = workflow steps, S = self-check (step 3), V = voice, R = what to return, SA to SF = section intros, N = "When not to act", K = keep list, SRC = source, AG = AGENTS.md, OB = removed upstream.

**v3 strength scale.** §1 to §5 "justify an edit on one sighting" (strong). Patterns marked *weak alone* need other tells in the same passage: §8 (one dash), §9, §10, §11, §21 and §25 ("a single description of the page"). All other patterns are unmarked. The source calls §22 "the most certain tell". The scale weighs evidence of machine authorship (BH-C03, X-DEAI). Each pattern row records it so the target skill can replace it with a content or frequency condition. The target skill should not turn it into a ban or a count of tells.

**Owner decisions applied.** De-AI rules are X-DEAI. English-specific rules go to EN unless their point is purely de-AI.

## Conflicts found

1. **Redundancy rule and the conventions that repeat on purpose.** BH-C01 says "Every sentence you keep must add something…". Technical text repeats on purpose: summaries, self-contained sections, repeated safety warnings. So do JA PREP and まとめ and ZH 总分总 and 小结. Resolution: BOTH, with an exception for repetition that serves navigation or the genre's structure.
2. **Sentence length.** BH-W4.3 says "Vary sentence length", but the same repo's AGENTS.md says "Keep sentences and paragraphs short" (BH-AG-W03), and JA has 一文一義. Resolution by genre: BH-W4.3 is PERSONAL and BH-AG-W03 is TECH.
3. **Adding a reaction vs adding no facts.** BH-V05 lets the editor "add a reaction where the writer would", while BH-W2.3 bans new facts. A first-person reaction is a fact about the writer. Resolution: PERSONAL, allowed only when the writer's notes, sample or conversation show the reaction.
4. **Author sample vs house style.** BH-V02 lets the sample override the rules; in technical text the house style guide outranks one writer's habits. Resolution: BH-V02 is PERSONAL (BH-K04 follows the same logic).
5. **Announcements and layout descriptions vs book and paper conventions.** BH-P04 removes run-ups and BH-P25c removes layout descriptions. JA tech books open chapters with 「本章では〜」, ZH docs use 「本文介绍……」, and EN, JA and ZH papers must reference every table in the text ("Table 1 shows", 「表1に示す」, 「如表1所示」). Resolution: conditional. Keep a scope sentence or in-text reference that names content or the takeaway.
6. **Dash ban vs punctuation norms.** BH-P08 bans dashes, but GB/T 15834 defines uses of the 破折号, JA uses the ダッシュ, and CMOS and AP allow em dashes. Resolution: X-DEAI. The owner's EN carve-out does not apply, because the rule's justification is purely machine authorship.
7. **Straight quotes vs Chinese typography.** BH-P21 prefers straight quotes, but GB/T 15834 prescribes “ ” in Chinese and JA uses 「」. Resolution: X-DEAI. The straight-quotes-in-code need goes to BH-R03.
8. **Missing subjects vs JA, ZH and technical conventions.** BH-P11 flags missing subjects. JA and ZH omit subjects by default (承前省略), and UI, error and commit text drops subjects by convention. Resolution: BOTH, conditional on the actor being ambiguous (the rubric's own example form).
9. **Repeated openings vs parallel steps and JA grammar.** BH-P07 conflicts with numbered steps, which open with imperative verbs, and its JA form is sentence-ending monotony (文末), not openings. Resolution: BOTH with a running-prose condition; the JA example goes to writing-ja.
10. **Fragments and triads vs ZH and JA rhetoric.** BH-P02b and BH-P06 conflict with ZH 排比 and 四字格 and with JA 体言止め. Resolution: content conditions (the run adds no claim; the item adds no distinct idea), not form bans.
11. **Acronym expansions.** BH-P19's example deletes acronym expansions, while BH-AG-W08 says to "explain technical terms". Resolution: writing-tech keeps expansions at first use, and the §19 example should not be copied as a model.
12. **Labeled lists vs lists that help.** BH-P19 (turn labeled lists into prose) can clash with BH-AG-W06 (use lists when they help) and with how technical readers scan. Resolution: apply BH-P19 only when the labels carry no information of their own.
13. **Descriptive vs evocative headings.** BH-P20e asks for descriptive headings, while personal essays use evocative ones. Resolution: TECH.
14. **One term per item vs varied wording.** BH-AG-W04 asks for one term per item. Upstream dropped synonym cycling as a human habit (c2c6cad), and personal writing varies words on purpose. Resolution: TECH.
15. **Double negatives.** BH-AG-W09 bans them, but ZH 双重否定 is a standard emphatic device, and JA 「〜ないわけではない」 and EN litotes are legitimate. Resolution: TECH, limited to instructions and specs.
16. **Section E intro vs its own patterns' guards.** BH-SE0 says "Remove these outright", while BH-P23b says to "State what the source does not show" and BH-P25 keeps source credits and caveats. This conflict is internal to the source. Resolution: drop BH-SE0 (X-SCOPE).
17. **"let me know" in §22 vs §26.** §22's watch list includes "let me know", while §26's model reply ends "Let me know if that works." This is internal. Resolution: BH-N03 (sign-offs on comments). Both rows are X-DEAI or BOTH, so nothing transfers.
18. **"as of [date]".** §23 flags "as of [date]", while technical docs date time-sensitive facts with it. Resolution: only the knowledge-cutoff disclaimer is X-DEAI (BH-P23a), and dating a fact stays allowed.
19. **§10 example vs its own guard.** "The plan is long term" drops a hyphen the dictionary guard would likely keep. Resolution: EN, with one named reference.
20. **"refers to" and "functions as".** BH-P18 replaces "refers to" and "functions as", which glossaries and definitions need. Resolution: EN, conditional on the longer verb adding meaning.
21. **"associated with" and "linked to".** BH-P14 flags them, but in technical text they can be the exact relationship (ORM association, linked library). Resolution: conditional.
22. **Process narration vs methods sections and essays.** BH-P25b says "cut the account of how you worked". Benchmarks, postmortems and papers need methods sections, and personal essays often tell the process as the story. Resolution: TECH, with a "reader needs it to judge or reproduce" condition.
23. **Era-bound references.** BH-K03 keeps slang, memes and in-jokes, while technical style guides avoid them. Resolution: PERSONAL.
24. **All caps vs spec keywords.** BH-P02c flags ALL CAPS, but specs use RFC 2119/BCP 14 keywords in capitals. Resolution: EN, with an exception for those keywords.
25. **No invention vs placeholder values.** BH-W2.3 bans invented specifics, but technical docs need invented example values. Resolution: conditional; the values must be clearly marked placeholders.
26. **Lead with the decision vs JA business-mail etiquette.** BH-P26 conflicts with the JA habit of cushioning and giving background first. Resolution: the salutation stays (BH-N03) and the decision follows it. Current JA business-writing advice (結論から) agrees, so the row stays BOTH.

## Language-specific examples to move

### EN (from the source)

- BH-P01: "not just / not only / not merely X, but Y", "it's not X, it's Y", "X rather than Y", the split form ("This does not mean X. It means Y."), and the clipped tail (", no guessing").
- BH-P02a: "That is the real win.", "That distinction matters.", "Read that again.", "Let that sink in.", "This shows the importance of…", "The message was clear:", "It was a lesson in patience."
- BH-P02b: "No aesthetic prior. No nostalgia."
- BH-P02c: ALL CAPS on a word, "every. single. day."
- BH-P03: the real question is, at its core, in reality, what really matters, fundamentally, the deeper issue, the heart of the matter, X is the Y of Z, X becomes a trap, X is not a tool but a mirror, the language of, the currency of, the architecture of.
- BH-P04: Let's dive in, let's explore, let's break this down, here's what you need to know, now let's look at, without further ado, heads up, quick note, Honestly?, Look, Here's the thing, The thing is, Let's be honest, Real talk.
- BH-P05: This isn't (mainly) about, I'm not saying, To be clear, Don't get me wrong, This is not to say, Some might say… but, A tempting approach would be, One might be tempted to, You might think… but, It would be easy to just.
- BH-P07: "She came. She saw. She conquered." (deliberate anaphora, kept).
- BH-P09: to be fair, it's also possible, could potentially, might arguably, in some cases it may, this is an inference; the kept hedges *perhaps* and *tends to*.
- BH-P10: high-quality, well-known, well-documented, long-term, real-time, client-facing; third-party and cross-functional (always hyphenated).
- BH-P11: "No configuration file needed. The results are preserved automatically."
- BH-P13: stands as a testament, a pivotal or crucial moment, plays a key role, marking or shaping the, underscores its importance, reflects a broader, enduring or lasting legacy, setting the stage for, evolving landscape, indelible mark, "Despite these challenges… continues to thrive", the headings "Challenges and Legacy", "Future Outlook" and "Awards and recognition", "the future looks bright", "exciting times ahead", "a step in the right direction".
- BH-P14: associated with, in association with, connected to, in connection with, linked to, tied to.
- BH-P15: highlighting, underscoring, emphasizing, ensuring, reflecting, symbolizing, contributing to, cultivating, fostering, encompassing, showcasing.
